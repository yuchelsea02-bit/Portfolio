01 — About

I'm a Media Studies student at UBC who thinks in images — bilingual, bicultural, and most at home where visual design meets research and story.

Raised between China and Canada, I make work that sits at the intersection of visual storytelling, user-centered design, and cultural identity — from double-exposure photography to Figma prototypes to posters seen by hundreds of students on campus. I care about the small decisions — a crop, a color, a transition — that make people actually feel something.

"It invites viewers to consider cultural heritage's dynamic and ever-changing nature in a globalized society."— from The Chinese Echo, artist statement
02 — Selected Work

Selected Work

01

The Chinese Echo

Fine-art photography series · double & triple exposure, Photoshop compositing

A personal photography series exploring the layered identity of Vancouver's Chinatown — and my own. Having grown up between China and Canada, I used double- and triple-exposure techniques to fuse self-portraits with the textures of the neighbourhood: a seafood market, an herbal shop, a Suzhou-style garden, the glow of the Chinatown gate. The result is a set of images about nostalgia, hybridity, and what gets lost and gained in translation between cultures.

02

La Poterie

Short documentary film · Director & Producer

▶ Watch on YouTube

A short, quiet film following the pottery-making process — kneading, wheel-throwing, shaping. As director and producer, I planned the shot list, storyboard, and lighting, and made deliberate calls on composition, pacing, and sequencing to let the texture of the clay and the patience of the craft carry the story.

03

Building Personas from Experience

UX research & design · Figma

Born from my own frustration with scheduling Lagree classes around a packed calendar, this personal project turns a real problem into a full UX solution. I ran market research and user interviews, synthesized the findings into a data-driven persona, and used Figma to design an end-to-end product experience — from problem statement to prototype.

04

Posters & Social Media Campaigns

Event & social design · Canva, Meta Business Suite — UBC CSSA, UGTS

As Art Design lead for the UBC CSSA Life Department and a marketing volunteer with the Unique Get Together Society (UGTS), I designed the posters and campaign materials that got people through the door — for a 300-person campus Halloween event, an accessible snowsports camp for youth with disabilities, and a peer research recruitment drive. At UGTS, a non-profit supporting Indigenous and marginalized communities in BC, I went further: managing two Facebook pages and one Instagram account, posting across hundreds of community groups, and acting as the sole point of contact between the volunteer team and the client — verifying every piece of content for accuracy and cultural sensitivity before it went out.

05

From Raw Data to Interactive Dashboard

Data analysis & visualization · Tableau, Excel, Tableau Prep Builder

Inspired by UBC's Making Research Accessible Initiative, I worked with a team to turn a dense academic study on housing eviction and violence among people who inject drugs in Vancouver into an accessible, interactive dashboard. I cleaned corrupted, inconsistent data in Excel and Tableau Prep Builder, defined an accessible colour palette guided by the "Do No Harm" framework, sketched chart concepts by hand, then built and presented a Tableau dashboard that lets a general audience filter and explore the findings for themselves.

06

On Bathroom Walls

UX & brand design · Figma, Procreate — UBC INFO 300

An archive network preserving the anonymous, ephemeral writing found on university bathroom stalls — confessions, jokes, cries for help — before it gets painted over. As the project's visual designer, I led the logo, typography, colour system, icon set, and a seven-page Figma prototype. The logo alone went through a full cycle of sketching in Procreate, group critique, and revision — from a busy collage of toilets and stall doors down to a single minimalist icon. I anchored the palette in a warm, unpolished burnt orange so the interface would feel respectful rather than over-designed, keeping the anonymity and rawness of the writing intact.
